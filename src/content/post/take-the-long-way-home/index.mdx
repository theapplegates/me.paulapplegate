A direct route has its uses. It gets us where we need to be. But occasionally, a route chosen for something other than speed can make an ordinary journey feel new.

A quieter street. A path through the trees. Ten minutes beside the water before heading back.

## Follow a little curiosity

Choose a small, familiar detour. Notice how a place changes when you arrive on foot, approach from another direction, or pass through at a different hour.

You do not need a grand destination to make space for a walk. The point can be the time itself.

## Bring less of the day with you

Put the to-do list away for a moment. Let the walk have its own pace. If an idea arrives, keep it; if nothing arrives, that is fine too.

## Come back with one thing

A color, a shape, the name of a tree to look up later. A small discovery is plenty. The long way home is not a challenge to complete. It is an invitation to notice what the shortcut leaves out.
